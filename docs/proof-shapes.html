One proof against a hundred: the shape of what depends on what

Each picture is a dominator tree drawn to the same rule: the root at the left, one column per step down, and a box's height is the share of that proof's lines that exist only to prove it. The archive is on top. Below it, the eight largest proofs among 3,000 Mathlib theorems drawn at random, which are all about L-functions, number fields and modular forms. Mathlib proofs are fans. The archive is a chain.

How much of the proof is still on the spine, step by step

One line per proof. In a Mathlib proof the mass leaves the spine at once, because the theorem itself joins many independent inputs. In the archive everything is passed down whole for three steps.

Six measures, one dot per proof

The archive is the blue marker; the tick is the Mathlib median. Hinges are theorems that carry at least 1% of the proof. A rewrite mark is a name ending in _alt, _fix, _bis or similar; primes are left out because Mathlib uses them as house style.
