The Riemann Hypothesis proof, on the record

Levent Alpöge released a Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, written with Claude, on 20 November 2026: 175,187 files, 17.4 million lines. This page is the public register of what has been done with it by Nervous Machine: built from source twice, checked against Lean's kernel, and measured. Nothing here is a reading of the mathematics; the reading guide is for the person who gives one.

Two pictures

Archive mapThe proof as a tree of what depends on what: one theorem at the top, the mass dividing below it, rewrite marks on the joins. 831 constants carrying at least 0.1% of the proof. Proof shapesThe archive beside the eight largest of 3,000 Mathlib proofs drawn at random, to one rule. Mathlib proofs are fans; the archive is a chain.

Who has said what in public

20 NovAbhishek Saha, automorphic formsA route through existing results in higher-rank automorphic forms rather than new theory; gives it a very good chance of being correct. An opinion on the shape, not a reading.
20 NovJ. L. Nielsen, physics and philosophy of scienceDisputes: reports the geometric layer's hypotheses are vacuous and concludes the archive proves nothing. The conclusion does not follow from the reports; a kernel-accepted term of this type is a proof whatever the intermediate names say.
23 NovLevent Alpöge, the claimantSays the 250,000-page figure was a joke, that he wrote no readable account and put everything into Lean, that the Lean is "full of garbage obviously, but compiles", that criticism of it is reasonable, and that he expects most of it to prove unnecessary. Changes nothing about the check; says how to read the size.
23 NovYochay Jerby, Holon Institute of TechnologyAccepts the claim; disputes what it achieves: the zeros were located through the primes, not the primes explained through the zeros. His account of the proof's top matches the structure trace exactly.
